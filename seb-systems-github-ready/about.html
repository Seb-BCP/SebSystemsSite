Seb's Approach

Simplicity, Speed & Stability

My aim is to make the systems your business relies on simple to learn, fast to use and stable in daily operation—so they save time without creating operational chaos.

The owners were understandably cautious about replacing systems that payroll, invoicing and daily operations depended on. The risk was not that they were unwilling to improve. The problem was that there was no obvious, low-risk way to modernise the systems without creating disruption.

When I first started at Blue Collar People, most of the major components of the business were run through paper systems. As I became more involved across the business, it really bugged me how often I had to write down the same information, then put it into a computer and cross-check it against the original. I knew there was a better way to do what was being done, but I did not know what that was at the time.

I began searching for software packages that could do what our systems did, but I ran into several problems. They were expensive, they did not quite do the same things we were already doing, and there was a real risk that they would not work after replacing an entire established system. I realised I was looking at the problem with the wrong approach. I was searching for a full, sweeping replacement when the existing system already contained years of useful operating knowledge.

I stopped looking for a wholesale replacement and started looking for the upgrade that would create the most value without disrupting the work around it. The existing system was a foundation to strengthen, not a problem to throw away. I asked myself a different question:

What is the smallest change that can remove the most wasted time without destabilising the business?

The first opportunity was the way employees registered, signed employment contracts and provided documentation. It was handled through paper forms, scanned documents and manual uploads into the employee records. We replaced that one step with online forms that collect the documents and transfer the information forward.

That first improvement taught me the principle I have continued to follow:

Evolve the system from a strong foundation rather than tearing it down and starting from scratch.

Every improvement is designed around three practical outcomes:

  • SimplicityThe process should feel intuitive, without an instruction manual. Someone new should be able to learn it quickly and use it with confidence.
  • SpeedThe system should do the work quickly, remove repeated handling and give people time back for the work that needs their attention.
  • StabilityThe change should strengthen daily operations, not introduce chaos or put the critical work of the business at risk.

Before a full transfer, the upgraded process runs side by side with the existing system in day-to-day work. That proves it works operationally, exposes issues while the current process is still in place and gives the team confidence before it becomes the new standard.

I do not replace what you have already built. I help you evolve it.

For other small companies

For small businesses, this is a practical, lower-risk way forward. You do not need to reinvent the wheel: each improvement delivers value in daily work while protecting the people, knowledge and processes the business already relies on.
